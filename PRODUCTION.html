📇 Outreach — everything you need to land guests

This is the engine of the show. Your vault strategy is right: start with the closest network nodes to practice, then use warm 2nd-degree intros to reach notable people. Below is the full playbook, then your outreach tasks.

The strategy in one line

Don't cold-DM famous people first (low reply rate). Interview the most reachable, interesting person in your first layer, do a great job, then ask every guest for 2 intros. World-class people cluster together — so a warm intro from guest #6 can reach someone you could never cold-email. This is how the network compounds.

Step 1 — Build the guest pipeline (a living list)

Make a simple tracker (Google Sheet or an Obsidian note) with columns: Name · How we know them · Why interesting · Best contact channel · Status · Last touch date · Who owns them. Statuses: IdeaResearching contactMessage sentRepliedScheduledRecordedDeclined. Seed it with 15 names this week — mix of easy first-layer people and 2–3 "dream" targets to work toward via intros.

Step 2 — Find anyone's contact info

Email (highest reply rate for notable people)

  • Guess the pattern: most orgs use first@, first.last@, or flast@ the company domain. From your vault: check old-company domains and university naming patterns.
  • Verify before sending: use a free email verifier (Hunter.io free tier, or paste the guessed address into Gmail "compose" and hover — a Google profile photo often confirms it's real). Tools: Hunter.io (free 25/mo), Clearbit Connect, Apollo.io free tier.
  • Academics: emails are usually public on their university department page or the last page of their papers (PDF). Find high-H-index researchers on Google Scholar, then their co-authors (your vault idea).

Social DMs (best for first-layer + creators)

  • Instagram, X/Twitter, TikTok, LinkedIn, Facebook. LinkedIn is strongest for professionals; X is strongest for tech/founders; IG/TikTok for creators.
  • Keep DMs short, specific, and human — see the template below.

Warm intros (the multiplier)

  • For any "dream" guest, list who you know that might know them (spouse, best friend, former colleague, per your vault). Ask that person for a forwardable intro — a 2-line blurb they can paste.

Step 3 — The messages (steal these)

Cold email / DM template

Subject: Your story on Goldilocks
Hi [Name] — I'm one of three people behind Goldilocks, a podcast about people's life stories. We're less interested in résumés and more in the actual path — the turns, the bets, the almost-didn't moments. [One specific, genuine sentence about why them — a detail that proves you did homework.] Would you be open to a relaxed 45–60 min conversation, recorded remotely? Totally flexible on timing. — [Your name]

Warm-intro request (to your mutual friend)

"Hey [friend] — we're starting a life-story podcast and I'd love to interview [target]. Would you be up for a quick intro? Here's a blurb you can forward: [paste 2-sentence blurb]. No worries if not!"

Follow-up (send once, 4–5 days later)

"Hi [Name], floating this back up in case it slipped by — totally understand if the timing's off. Happy to work around your schedule."

📣 Marketing — build the audience

You don't need a big audience to start (founder philosophy: bet on intent, not traction) — but marketing is how episodes find listeners and how guests see you're legit. Build the machine in parallel with recording.

The core idea: clips-first, show-second

Almost nobody discovers a podcast by browsing Spotify. They find a 60-second clip on TikTok / Reels / Shorts / X, then go subscribe. So the real marketing product is short vertical clips from each episode. Nikki's editing lane feeds this directly — one interview = one full episode + 3–5 clips.

Channel setup (do once)

  • Handle: lock the same @goldilocks-style handle on Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, and X so you're consistent. Check availability on all before committing.
  • Profiles: logo as avatar, one-line description ("Life-story interviews — famous and unknown"), link to the Spotify/Apple show.
  • YouTube: create a channel even if audio-only — upload episodes as audio-with-cover-art or full video; it's the #2 podcast discovery engine after Spotify.
  • Link-in-bio: free Linktree/Beacons pointing to all listen links + "be a guest" form.

Content engine (repeatable per episode)

For every episode published, produce:

  • 🎬 3–5 vertical clips (30–60s) of the best moments — captioned (auto-captions in Descript/CapCut), hook in first 2 seconds.
  • 🖼️ 1 audiogram (waveform + quote) for feeds that aren't video-heavy.
  • ✍️ 1 quote graphic in Canva using brand red/gold.
  • 📝 Show notes + 3–5 pull quotes for the episode description and a caption bank.
  • 📌 Post the clips across TikTok / Reels / Shorts / X over the week following release (don't dump all at once).

Tools: Descript or CapCut (free) for clips + captions; Canva (free) for graphics/audiograms; Buffer or Later (free tier) to schedule posts across platforms at once.

Growth tactics that actually work early

  • Guest amplification: send each guest 2–3 ready-to-post clips + captions. Their share to their audience is your #1 growth lever — make it zero-effort for them.
  • Post consistently, not perfectly: 3–5 clips/week beats 1 polished video/month.
  • Engage before you broadcast: comment genuinely on the accounts of people in your niche; borrowed reach.
  • Cross-post to communities where the guest's world lives (relevant subreddits, Discords, group chats) — only where it's welcome and relevant.
  • Email/DM list: collect emails from day one (free tier of any provider); a small owned list beats rented algorithm reach.
