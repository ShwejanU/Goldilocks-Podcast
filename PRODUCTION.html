📇 Outreach — everything you need to land guests

This is the engine of the show. Start with the closest network nodes to practice, then use warm 2nd-degree intros to reach notable people.

The strategy in one line

Don't cold-DM famous people first (low reply rate). Interview the most reachable, interesting person in your first layer, do a great job, then ask every guest for 2 intros. World-class people cluster together — a warm intro from guest #6 can reach someone you could never cold-email.

Step 1 — Build the guest pipeline

Tracker columns: Name · How we know them · Why interesting · Best contact channel · Status · Last touch date · Owner. Statuses: IdeaResearching contactMessage sentRepliedScheduledRecordedDeclined. Seed 15 names this week.

Step 2 — Find anyone's contact info

Email (highest reply rate for notable people)

  • Guess the pattern: first@, first.last@, or flast@ the company domain.
  • Verify: Hunter.io (free 25/mo), Clearbit Connect, Apollo.io free tier; or check for a Google profile photo on the guessed address.
  • Academics: emails are usually public on university department pages or the last page of papers. Find high-H-index researchers on Google Scholar, then their co-authors.

Social DMs

  • Instagram, X/Twitter, TikTok, LinkedIn, Facebook. LinkedIn strongest for professionals, X for tech/founders, IG/TikTok for creators. Keep DMs short and specific.

Warm intros (the multiplier)

  • For any dream guest, list who you know that might know them (spouse, best friend, former colleague). Ask for a forwardable intro blurb.

Step 3 — Messages (steal these)

Cold email / DM

Subject: Your story on Goldilocks
Hi [Name] — I'm one of three people behind Goldilocks, a podcast about people's life stories. We're less interested in résumés and more in the actual path — the turns, the bets, the almost-didn't moments. [One specific, genuine sentence about why them.] Would you be open to a relaxed 45–60 min conversation, recorded remotely? Totally flexible on timing. — [Your name]

Warm-intro request

"Hey [friend] — we're starting a life-story podcast and I'd love to interview [target]. Would you be up for a quick intro? Here's a blurb you can forward: [2 sentences]. No worries if not!"

Follow-up (once, 4–5 days later)

"Hi [Name], floating this back up in case it slipped by — totally understand if timing's off. Happy to work around your schedule."

📣 Marketing — build the audience

You don't need a big audience to start (bet on intent, not traction) — but marketing is how episodes find listeners.

The core idea: clips-first, show-second

Almost nobody discovers a podcast by browsing Spotify. They find a 60-second clip on TikTok / Reels / Shorts / X, then subscribe. One interview = one full episode + 3–5 clips. Nikki's editing lane feeds this directly.

Channel setup (do once)

  • Handle: lock the same @handle on Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, X — check availability first.
  • Profiles: logo as avatar, one-line description, link to the show.
  • YouTube: create a channel even audio-only — #2 podcast discovery engine after Spotify.
  • Link-in-bio: free Linktree/Beacons pointing to listen links + "be a guest" form.

Content engine (per episode)

  • 🎬 3–5 vertical clips (30–60s), captioned, hook in first 2 seconds.
  • 🖼️ 1 audiogram (waveform + quote).
  • ✍️ 1 quote graphic in Canva, brand red/gold.
  • 📝 Show notes + 3–5 pull quotes.
  • 📌 Post clips across the week following release — don't dump all at once.

Tools: Descript or CapCut (free) for clips/captions; Canva (free) for graphics; Buffer/Later (free tier) to schedule.

Growth tactics that work early

  • Guest amplification: send each guest 2–3 ready-to-post clips + captions — their share is your #1 lever.
  • Post consistently, not perfectly: 3–5 clips/week beats 1 polished video/month.
  • Engage before broadcasting: comment genuinely in your niche.
  • Cross-post to relevant communities only where welcome.
  • Owned email list from day one beats rented algorithm reach.
